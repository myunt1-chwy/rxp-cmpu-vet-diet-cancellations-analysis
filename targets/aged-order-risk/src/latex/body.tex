We look at the chance of an order becoming an aged order in this
section. Here, we build a survival curve. Survival means that an order
has yet to encounter an accepted document upload or a cancellation.
After day 10, nearly all cancellations are system-created aged-order
cancellations: \PostDayTenAgingShare{} (\PostDayTenAgingLines{} of
\PostDayTenTotalLines{} lines) are recorded as \emph{Aged Order} or
\emph{Prescription Issue}, which pharmacists use when no prescription
exists by day 10, and \PostDayTenCustomerShare{} are customer
cancellations (Table~\ref{tab:post-day-10-cancellation-reasons}).
Therefore, for an order with no accepted document upload or
cancellation by day 3, the chance of becoming an aged order is about
50\% (28.91\%/55.80\%, from Figure~\ref{fig:first-order-line-survival}).
Because a few uploads and other cancellations still occur after day 10,
this ratio slightly overstates the aged-order risk.

Figure~\ref{fig:first-order-line-survival} shows the probability that a first-order
line is still awaiting an accepted upload or cancellation after order placement.
