\section{Executive Summary}\label{abstract}


In CMPU (Customer Must Upload) vet diet orders shipped or canceled
between May 1, 2026 and July 31, 2026, 38,314 orders were canceled due
to aging. In this context, aging means the time between order creation
and order shipment or cancellation exceeded 11 days. CMPU orders are
particular in  that the customers must upload the
prescription documents themselves for approval.

The number of aging orders was  about 10\% of the orders that were
actually shipped with an estimated loss of 3,5 million, again about
10\% of the net sales of the actually shipped orders (Table
\ref{tab:financial-opportunity-cancellations}).

In the following seven days after a cancellation, 2,316 out of 30,382
customers return to shop the same part number for the same pet and
successfully reorder for a total net sales of 221 thousand (Table
\ref{tab:financial-opportunity-reorder-windows}) recovering 7.6\% of
the loss. After 28 days, Chewy recovers only about 20\% of the
estimated net sales. By 70 days, we have only 26\% of the customers
shopping the same product for the same pet. The attrition is worse for
customers whose first CMPU order was the aged order; retention is
7.2\% and 21.72\% respectively for 7 and 70 days (11,500 customers,
see Table \ref{tab:financial-opportunity-new-upload-follow-up}).  The
worst segment is the new to vet diet customers; and their 70 day
reorder rate is only 15\% (5K customers, see Table
\ref{tab:financial-opportunity-new-vet-diet-follow-up}). The rate of
reorder in Chewy of these cohorts follows the same order 61\%, 58\%
and 50\%. A subsequent study will analyze longer time frames.

All of the customers seem to have contacted Chewy, most of them at the
creation of the order (Table
\ref{tab:aged-customer-contacts-by-cancellation-day}). Some of them
have contacted Chewy on multiple days (Table
\ref{tab:aged-customer-contact-day-frequency}). People who
contacted closer to the aged order cancellation date have a higher
reorder rate (Table \ref{tab:contact-window-same-pet-part-reorders}).

The study analyzes first orders and order lines associated with CMPU
prescriptions. Details can be found in Section
\ref{app:aged-order-analysis}.  The main findings are that highly
likely aged orders are caused by customers not uploading their
prescription documents; and that after 3 days there is 50\%+ chance
that a CMPU order becomes an aged order.

That the attrition rate is higher for customers who encounter this
first time hints that uploading the prescription is an onus on the
customer. The customer might not be uploading the prescription because
it might be hard to obtain from the vet in person, it might be hard to
upload for some reason ( the Chewy web site or Chewy app not working for the customer),
or once the customer goes to the vet, they have the ability procure the
product on site or nearby.

One suggestion to increase the upload rate (communication with
Courtney Gleede) is to send reminder emails to customer. At this
point, Chewy does not contact customers if there is a chance that
their order might become aged. There is no Chewy relevant outbound contact data for a
comparative/causal analysis. The only contact happens when the
customer is notified that their order has been canceled.  Assuming
that people who receive an email before cancellation, react similarly
to the cohort that reorders within 7 days after cancellation, we
expect that out of 30K people considered in the opportunity study with
aged cancellations, 2.3K would react before order cancellation
corresponding to 221K sales. The best effort guess is that Chewy
prevents people from skipping one reorder for this cohort for an
additional gain of 221K net sales.  The upper bound on the best effort
estimate is 390K corresponding to the 14 day cohort. This represents
about 1\% increase net sales for CMPU vet diet orders.

The effect of reminder emails on long term retention is an open
question. Uploading a prescription is still an onus with or without
a reminder.

A controlled experiment where Chewy sends reminder emails to get a
better understanding of the entitlement regarding short term gain is
suggested as next steps.
