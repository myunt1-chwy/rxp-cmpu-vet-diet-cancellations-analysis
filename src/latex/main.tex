\section{Executive Summary}\label{abstract}

In CMPU (Customer Must Pickup and Upload) vet diet orders require that
the customer pick up the their prescriptions and upload them to Chewy
to get approval as a result of the approving vets preference.

This order group suffers from high cancellation rates; the top
cancellation reason begin ``aged order''; an automatic cancellation
that happens if the order has not been shipped or otherwise been
canceled by the customer after ten days from order creation. This type
of cancellation is followed by explicit cancellations by the customer
(``customer initiated cancellation'' and ``customer request''). The glaring
issue is that cancellation reasons do not provide actionable root
causes and require further investigative actions and analyses.
Possible root causes are; the inconvenience of picking up the
prescription, issues in uploading the vet prescription, wrong order,
ability to fulfill the order at the vet.

This report focuses on aged order cancellations. Among CMPU vet diet
orders shipped or canceled between May 1, 2026 and July 31, 2026,
38,314 orders were canceled due to aging. The number of aging orders
was about 10\% of the orders that were actually shipped with an
estimated loss of 3,5 million, again about 10\% of the net sales of
the actually shipped orders (Table
\ref{tab:financial-opportunity-cancellations}). We note that customer
initiated cancellations and customer requested cancellations combined
comprise a comparable-sized loss.

Compared to the whole customer group, the percent of the customers
whose first order was the aged vet diet order that return to shop for
vet diets is significantly lower. In the following seven days after a
cancellation, 2,316 out of 30,382 customers return to shop the same
part number for the same pet and successfully reorder for a total net
sales of 221 thousand (Table
\ref{tab:financial-opportunity-reorder-windows}) recovering 7.6\% of
the loss. After 28 days, Chewy recovers only about 20\% of the
estimated net sales. By 70 days, we have only 26\% of the customers
shopping the same product for the same pet. The attrition is worse for
customers whose first CMPU order was the aged order; retention is
7.2\% and 21.72\% respectively for 7 and 70 days (11,500 customers,
see Table \ref{tab:financial-opportunity-new-upload-follow-up}).  The
worst segment is the new to vet diet customers; and their 70 day
reorder rate is only 15\% (5K customers, see Table
\ref{tab:financial-opportunity-new-vet-diet-follow-up}). The rate of
reorder in Chewy of these cohorts follows the same order 61\%, 58\%
and 50\%.

All of the customers seem to have contacted Chewy, most of them at the
creation of the order (Table
\ref{tab:aged-customer-contacts-by-cancellation-day}). Some of them
have contacted Chewy on multiple days (Table
\ref{tab:aged-customer-contact-day-frequency}). People who
contacted closer to the aged order cancellation date have a higher
reorder rate (Table \ref{tab:contact-window-same-pet-part-reorders}).

Most (> 95\%) of the prescriptions of CMPU aged orders do not have a
prescription document uploaded (Table \ref{tab:study-population-counts}).  Of those prescriptions with uploads, the time between order creation and upload does not significantly differ from the rest of the study group
(Figure \ref{fig:entity-upload-vs-order-creation}). Less than 1\% of the uploaded prescriptions for aged orders get accepted and these are mostly accepted late; after 10 days (Figure \ref{fig:entity-upload-state-time-aged}). Finally, Chewy can expect that if the customer does not upload a prescription in 3 days after order creation; there is more than 50\% chance that the order will be canceled as an aged order (Figure \ref{fig:first-order-line-survival}).


So what can be done to reduce aged orders?  One suggestion to increase
the upload rate (communication with Courtney Gleede) is to send
reminder emails to customer. At this point, Chewy does not contact
customers in case there is a chance that their order might become
aged. There is no Chewy relevant out-bound contact data for a
comparative/causal analysis. The only contact happens when the
customer is notified that their order has been canceled.  Assuming
that people who receive an email before cancellation, react similarly
to the cohort that reorders within 7 days after cancellation, we
expect that out of 30K people considered in the opportunity study with
aged cancellations, 2.3K would react before order cancellation
corresponding to 221K sales. The best effort guess is that Chewy
prevents people from skipping one reorder for this cohort for an
additional gain of 221K net sales.  The upper bound on the best effort
estimate is 390K corresponding to the 14 day cohort. This represents
about 1\% increase in net sales for CMPU vet diet orders.

The effect of reminder emails on long term retention is an open
question. Picking up a prescription and uploading a prescription is
still an onus with or without a getting a reminder.

\subsection{Suggested Next Steps}
\begin{enumerate}
\item  The root cause of aged orders and the other customer initiated/requested cancellations is not known. We know that highly likely they did not upload their prescriptions but why they have not uploaded is still unknown. In order to get more insight into the root causes; Chewy should
\begin{enumerate}
  \item contact customers who had aged orders ad customer
    initiated/requested orders in person or via AI (AI calls or
    emails) to understand the reasons of why customers have not
    uploaded their prescriptions and any other difficulty they may
    have encountered in the process.  Chewy should focus on first time
    vet diet customers, as their retention is the worst.
  \item analyze the customer contact logs to see if there is more
    insight to be gained.
\end{enumerate}
\item A controlled experiment where Chewy sends reminder emails to
    get a better understanding of the entitlement regarding short term
    gain is suggested. The suggestion is to pick a cohort of new vet
    diet customers who need to upload their prescriptions, randomly
    split it in half and send an email to one half to observe if Chewy
    can reduce the aged orders in the treatment group (customers to
    whom an email is sent) compared to the control ( no email sent).
\end{enumerate}
