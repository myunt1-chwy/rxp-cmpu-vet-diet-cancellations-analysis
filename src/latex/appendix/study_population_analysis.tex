\subsection{Aged Order Delay  Analysis}\label{app:aged-order-analysis}
An order becomes an aged order when canceled automatically after the
time between order creation and time of shipment or any other
cancellation exceeds 11 days. A detailed breakdown of cancellation
reasons can be found in Figure \ref{fig:first-order-lines-customer-upload}
for the study population.

The population of orders for this study consists of first orders and
first order lines for prescriptions as we would like to analyze issues at
prescription upload and approval.

For this analysis; 95,073 vet diet prescriptions created between
August 15, 2026 and October 15, 2026 were used with
\verb|CUSTOMER_UPLOAD| as the approval channel. This corresponds to
87,724 distinct orders and 95,073 order lines. The 24,408 orders
described in \verb|OMS| with the cancellation reason
\verb|AGED_ORDER| constitute the  aged orders leading to 26,443 line
items, 26,443 corresponding prescriptions and 20,587 customers.

The difference in counts between first orders and order lines and all
orders and order lines is shown in Table
{tab:first-all-order-cancellations}.


Factors that might be contributing to an order aging are: delay in
prescription upload by the customer, delay in processing the upload,
and delay in approving the prescription after the uploaded
prescription document is accepted.

The data for uploaded documents/entities does not cover all the
prescriptions, the coverage is about 31\% in this study. The number of
prescription documents uploaded for 26,443 aged order prescriptions is
782 (~3\%) compared to 33,336 uploads for 95,073 prescriptions overall
(~35\%) pointing to a severe non-response by the customers. See Table
\ref{tab:study-population-counts}.

Only 6 aged order related prescription were approved out of 52
accepted/782 uploaded (See Table
\ref{tab:study-population-prescription-decisions} for comparison with
the rest of the order line population.) The median time for these
prescriptions to become accepted was 10 days after upload, as a result
missing the aged order cancellation deadline (Figure
\ref{fig:entity-upload-state-time-aged}).  For comparison, the
distributions of the time difference between upload of the documents
and their final states are in Figure
\ref{fig:entity-upload-state-time-overall} for all entities.
Customers seem to upload at similar delays after order creation for
overall and aged order prescriptions (Figure
\ref{fig:entity-upload-vs-order-creation}).

From the available entity upload data, it takes less than a minute to
approve an accepted prescription document see Figure
\ref{fig:accepted-upload-to-approval-cumulative}.

We look at the chance of an order becoming an aged order in Section
\ref{subsec:survival-analysis}. Here, we build a survival
curve. Survival means that an order has yet to encounter a document
upload or cancellation. One can see in Figure
\ref{fig:first-order-line-survival} that after day 3, the change of an
order becoming an aged order is about ~50\% (28.90%/55.80%).
